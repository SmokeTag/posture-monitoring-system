Musculoskeletal disorders affect most office workers and are associated with poor postures held for long periods. This work develops an embedded system that detects harmful sitting posture and alerts the user through vibration. A chair with eight force-sensing resistors measures how body weight is distributed, and a collar with an inertial sensor measures the orientation of the upper back. The chair fuses both sources, calibrated to each user's upright posture, and sends the alert to the collar over radio. In tests with eight participants, each evaluated outside parameter tuning, the system alerted in 58 of 62 harmful-posture segments, while alerting during 3\,\% of upright or reclined time. Fusion removed most false alarms when reclining and enabled the detection of the hips sliding forward. At a public exhibition, more than 150 visitors used the prototype with consistent detection, and a 600\,mAh battery kept the collar running for about 20\,h. The results indicate the technical feasibility of the approach; isolated head protraction remains the main limitation.

\textbf{Keywords:} Posture monitoring. Ergonomics. Force-sensing resistors. Inertial measurement unit. Sensor fusion.
